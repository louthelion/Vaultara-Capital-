SBA loans enter fiscal 2027 under a tighter credit box: four rules that got stricter on October 1

The U.S. Small Business Administration opened fiscal year 2027 on October 1, 2026 with a second round of tighter underwriting rules for its 7(a) and 504 loan programs, as American Banker reported on October 9, 2026. A “credit box” is the set of standards lenders use to decide which applications qualify — the box this story describes is the one every SBA application now has to fit inside. The new rules land on top of an earlier revision from June 2025, which reinstated a 10% equity injection for startup and change-of-ownership financing and tightened personal-guarantee and small-dollar loan rules.

Four things got harder for borrowers. First, anyone seeking more than $3 million for a business acquisition must obtain a CPA-prepared quality-of-earnings report — an independent review of whether the target company’s reported profits are real and repeatable. Second, lenders must apply stricter debt-service requirements, meaning the business’s cash flow must cover its loan payments by a wider margin. Third, non-cash sources such as a seller note can cover no more than half of the required 10% equity injection: on a $1 million purchase, the injection is $100,000, and at most $50,000 of it can be non-cash. Fourth, underwriting decisions must rest on trailing earnings — the business’s actual historical results — rather than on forward-looking projections.

The agency’s stated aim is restoring prudent lending standards after a looser period, and the effect is already visible in the numbers: American Banker reported that fiscal 2026 lending fell across both flagship programs, with 7(a) dollar volume down by a double-digit percentage. For a Florida owner, the preparation lesson is concrete. Clean up the trailing financials before applying, because history is now the application. Document where the cash part of your equity injection comes from. Run the debt-service math on past earnings, not on the growth you plan — if the deal only works on projections, it does not work under these rules.
