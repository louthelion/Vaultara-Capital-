Credit Basics

What Credit Is

Credit is a record of how a person or business borrows and manages obligations. That record can include payment behavior, balances, limits, account history, inquiries, and signs of financial responsibility.

What to remember
  • Credit reflects patterns over time, not one isolated number.
  • Reports may contain different accounts and information.
  • Understanding the record should come before applying.
Related next step: Credit readiness →
Credit Basics

Why Credit Matters

Credit may influence funding and loan decisions, interest rates, available limits, repayment terms, and whether a person or business appears ready for a new obligation.

What to remember
  • Different providers use different review standards.
  • Stronger preparation does not guarantee approval.
  • Rates, limits, and terms can vary by profile and product.
Related next step: Explore credit guidance →
Personal Credit

Payment History

Payment history shows whether obligations were paid as agreed. On-time payments can support a record of responsibility, while late or missed payments may reduce readiness and remain visible for a period of time.

What to remember
  • Know each due date and required minimum payment.
  • Contact the provider early if a payment problem develops.
  • One habit cannot guarantee a particular score result.
Related next step: Review credit readiness →
Personal Credit

Credit Utilization

Utilization compares revolving balances with available credit limits. High balances can signal heavier reliance on credit and may affect how ready a profile appears for another obligation.

What to remember
  • Balances can change before reporting dates.
  • A lower balance can create more available room.
  • No single utilization target guarantees an outcome.
Related next step: Learn about your profile →
Personal Credit

Credit Inquiries

A hard inquiry can occur when a provider checks credit for an application. Applying repeatedly within a short period can create multiple inquiries and may make a profile look less carefully planned.

What to remember
  • Ask what type of inquiry may occur before applying.
  • Compare the purpose and fit of an account first.
  • Avoid submitting applications without a clear plan.
Related next step: Prepare before applying →
Business Credit

Personal Credit vs Business Credit

Personal credit is connected to an individual. Business credit is associated with a company. Depending on the product and business stage, a provider may review one, the other, or both.

What to remember
  • A business entity does not automatically create business credit.
  • Owner information or a personal guarantee may still matter.
  • Keep personal and company records organized separately.
Related next step: Business credit guidance →
Business Credit

How Business Credit Starts

A credible foundation begins with consistent company details: legal name, address, phone, bank account, registrations, and records. A thoughtful vendor or account strategy can then support the company’s profile.

What to remember
  • Use the same business information across records.
  • Confirm accounts actually report when reporting matters.
  • Choose vendors and accounts for a real business purpose.
Related next step: Build a business credit foundation →
Business Credit

Business Banking Basics

A dedicated business bank account helps separate company deposits, expenses, and records from personal activity. Clear banking records can make bookkeeping and future preparation easier.

What to remember
  • Route business income and expenses consistently.
  • Reconcile transactions and keep supporting records.
  • A bank account alone does not establish funding eligibility.
Related next step: Organize business credit →
Funding Readiness

Funding Readiness Basics

Before asking for funding, a business should understand its purpose, amount, timeline, repayment responsibility, operating history, cash flow, credit considerations, and available documents.

What to remember
  • Preparation should happen before an application.
  • Match the request to a real business need.
  • No readiness checklist can promise funding.
Related next step: Funding guidance →
Funding Readiness

Funding Purpose

A business should know exactly why it needs funds—such as equipment, inventory, marketing, working capital, or expansion—and how the proposed use could support operations.

What to remember
  • Define the use, amount, and timing.
  • Connect the request to a practical business plan.
  • Understand how repayment would fit cash flow.
Related next step: Clarify your funding goal →
Funding Readiness

Revenue and Cash Flow

Revenue shows money earned, while cash flow reflects money moving in and out. Providers may consider deposits, expenses, consistency, existing obligations, and whether the business can manage repayment.

What to remember
  • Revenue and available cash are not the same thing.
  • Keep deposits and expenses easy to explain.
  • Review repayment pressure under realistic conditions.
Related next step: Assess funding readiness →
Documents

Documents to Prepare

Depending on the request, useful records may include formation papers, bank statements, invoices, tax records, identification, a business plan, licenses, contracts, and other business records.

What to remember
  • Requirements vary by service and provider.
  • Keep current files organized and readable.
  • Share sensitive documents only through an appropriate process.

Business Formation

Business Formation Basics

A clean business setup creates an organized foundation before growth. The entity type, state filing, tax registration, licenses, banking, ownership records, and operating practices should fit the business.

What to remember
  • Formation is more than filing a name.
  • Understand ongoing state and tax responsibilities.
  • Get licensed legal or tax advice when needed.
Related next step: Formation guidance →
Business Formation

Entity Information

Core company information can include the legal business name, address, ownership, EIN, state filing, contact details, and industry. Consistency helps records connect to the correct company.

What to remember
  • Review state and federal records for accuracy.
  • Update changes through the proper channels.
  • Use consistent details on banking and applications.
Related next step: Organize your entity →
Mistakes to Avoid

Common Funding Mistakes

Common mistakes include applying too quickly, requesting money without a defined purpose, keeping poor records, mixing finances, overlooking repayment, and expecting unrealistic amounts or timing.

What to remember
  • Pause and prepare before submitting applications.
  • Use current records and realistic assumptions.
  • Treat every funding decision as a repayment decision.
Related next step: Prepare for funding →
Mistakes to Avoid

Common Credit Mistakes

High balances, missed payments, repeated applications, ignored reports, and mixed personal and business activity can make it harder to understand the profile or prepare responsibly.

What to remember
  • Review information instead of guessing.
  • Build consistent payment and recordkeeping habits.
  • Do not open accounts only to chase a score.
