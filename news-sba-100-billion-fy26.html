SBA closes fiscal 2026 with $100 billion delivered and its third-highest loan volume ever

On November 2, 2026, the U.S. Small Business Administration announced that it closed fiscal year 2026 — the year that ended September 30 — with $100 billion in capital and related assistance delivered through its flagship programs. The core of that total is guaranteed lending: the SBA backed 62,205 loans under its 7(a) and 504 programs, totaling $39.5 billion. The agency calls that the third-highest lending volume in its history, building on a record-setting fiscal 2025.

The split shows where the money went. The 7(a) program, the SBA’s general-purpose loan guarantee used for working capital, equipment and acquisitions, approved 56,130 loans totaling $32.4 billion. The 504 program, aimed at owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment, approved 6,075 loans totaling $7.1 billion — together, about 1,350 small-business loans a week. Beyond the two loan programs, the Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) program closed the year with a record $60 billion in combined private capital and SBA leverage, the SBA supported more than 9,700 startups with $6.5 billion in financing, and it disbursed $192 million in disaster loans.

The number deserves one piece of context before any owner reads it as a forecast. The year included a 43-day federal shutdown that halted SBA lending; the agency estimates it could not guarantee roughly $170 million in loans each business day of that freeze, about $5 billion in financing that otherwise could have been approved. For a Florida business owner, the practical reading is narrower and more useful: the country’s main small-business lending door is open, busy, and processing at near-record pace — which means the applications that move are the prepared ones, with a clear purpose for the money, organized records, and a repayment story a lender can follow.
