Business Formation

Understand the records, separation, and ongoing responsibilities involved in creating a business entity.

← Back to Learning Center

Use this guide to understand the subject before choosing a service or submitting an application. The information is educational and should be considered alongside your own records, responsibilities, and professional advice when needed.

Choose a structure deliberately

Business structures can differ in ownership, liability, management, filing, and tax treatment. Formation should reflect the actual business need. Legal and tax questions should be directed to appropriately licensed professionals.

Keep core records secure

Maintain approved formation documents, governing documents, ownership records, EIN confirmation, licenses, amendments, and state correspondence. Record filing and renewal dates so the company can remain in good standing.

Set up dedicated business banking

Banks may request formation records, EIN confirmation, identification, and ownership information. Use the business account for genuine company income and expenses, reconcile it regularly, and document owner contributions or withdrawals.

Maintain separation and consistency

Use the legal business name and stable contact information consistently. Avoid mixing personal and company transactions. Update material changes with the relevant state, tax authority, bank, insurer, licensing body, and other record keepers.

What to remember

  • Formation creates ongoing responsibilities, not just an initial filing.
  • Keep entity, EIN, ownership, and state records together.
  • Separate personal and business money and records.
  • Use licensed legal or tax help when the decision requires it.
