Time-sensitive: Florida’s zero-interest emergency bridge loans for storm-hit businesses close November 25

FloridaCommerce has activated the Florida Small Business Emergency Bridge Loan Program, making $10 million available to small businesses impacted by Hurricane Isaias. The loans are open to businesses in 30 designated counties, including Escambia, Santa Rosa, Hillsborough, Pinellas and Pasco. A bridge loan is exactly what its name says: short-term money that carries a business across the gap between a disaster and the longer-term recovery funding — an insurance payout, a federal disaster loan, or a commercial loan — that takes longer to arrive.

The terms are as plain as state lending gets. Eligible small businesses, including sole proprietors, can apply for up to $100,000, and agriculture and aquaculture businesses can apply for up to $150,000. The loans are zero-interest and short-term, and they are loans, not grants: every dollar must be repaid. FloridaCommerce runs the program with Florida First Capital Finance Corporation as its fiscal administrator. The deadline is the sharp edge of this story: applications are open through November 25, 2026, or until all funds are exhausted, whichever comes first.

For a Florida owner whose business took storm damage, the useful lesson is that bridge money rewards the prepared. The businesses that move first are the ones that can already show what the storm cost them — damage records, revenue interruption, and organized financials — rather than starting that paperwork while the window is closing. If your business was hurt by Hurricane Isaias and sits in one of the designated counties, the calendar matters more than the brochure: five days remain from the date of this briefing. Applications run through FloridaCommerce’s Disaster Recovery program pages at FloridaJobs.org.
